# v0.4 findings: noise, imperfect comparators and firm size

25 November 2026. Synthetic development experiment; no empirical validation, holdout or live collection. Pre-execution plan: `experiment-plan-v04.md`.

## Question and short answer

Question: do the v0.3 indicator rules still separate AI-caused staffing losses from null histories when the comparator has its own demand fluctuations, published hours carry reporting error, and the organisation has 100 rather than 10 workers?

Short answer, within this model: **mostly yes at the 10% cutoff, no at 5%.** With i.i.d. quarterly demand shocks of 3%, noise adds no false paid-hours alerts at 10%; at 6% it adds about 1–2% of null histories. The dominant source of false alarms is still the planted hidden-confounder case, not noise. At a 5% cutoff, noise adds false alerts in 7–10% of nulls. The hiring indicator remains weak under every setting. Firm size changes little, and not in the direction predicted.

## What changed from v0.3

New files only; v0.1–v0.3 code and outputs are untouched and their hashes are checked before and after the run.

- `employer_model_v04.py`: adds `size` (everything scales; natural departures are 10% of initial staff per quarter) and multiplicative demand shocks `1 + ε`, ε ~ Normal(0, σ_d) truncated at ±3σ_d. The treated organisation's with-AI and no-AI paths share one shock series, so causal truth stays defined; the comparator gets an independent series.
- `employer_measurement_v04.py`: published paid hours (treated and comparator) carry multiplicative error, σ_m. Headcount, hires and layoffs are exact. The indicator (`assess`) and evaluator (`evaluate`) are imported from v0.3 unchanged. No rule was retuned.
- `test_noise_v04.py`: seven tests, including exact reproduction of v0.3 at size 10 with no noise, scaling, accounting under shocks, shared/independent shocks, hours-only reporting error, and the future-evidence boundary.

Grid: 17 structural cases × sizes {10, 100} × σ_d {0, 0.03, 0.06} × σ_m {0, 0.03} × cutoffs {5, 10, 20%}; 40 seeds for noisy cells, one seed for noise-free cells. Immediate publication, cumulative flows, evaluated at q12. 36 cells, 2,754 simulated histories. Full metrics: `outputs/noise-v04/run-001/metrics.csv`.

## Predictions versus results

| Prediction | Result |
|---|---|
| P1: size 10, no noise reproduces v0.3 | **Confirmed** exactly: every case, path and the primary table. |
| P2: size 100, no noise gives the same table | **Confirmed at table level, failed at history level.** `external_demand_slump` with AI gives 8 workers at size 10 but 82 (not 80) at size 100: core work needs 4.2 workers, and only the larger firm can staff close to that. Classifications are unchanged. Found in pre-run tests; recorded in the plan. |
| P3: demand noise raises hours false alerts, most at 5% | **Confirmed, but smaller than expected at 10%** (table below). |
| P4: reporting error separates hours from headcount | **Confirmed.** At 5%, σ_m = 0.03 raises hours false alerts from 12% to 18% while headcount stays at 12%. At 10% the gap is 13% vs 12%. |
| P5: size 100 has fewer false alerts than size 10 | **Not supported.** Size 100 is similar or slightly worse (e.g. 17% vs 15% at σ_d = 0.06, 10%), and hours recall at σ_d = 0.03 is lower (78% vs 86%). Whole-worker rounding in a 10-person firm absorbs small shocks; a 100-person firm passes them through. |

Consequence for an earlier review comment: the suggestion that the q12 hiring decay (2 of 9) is "partly a firm-size artefact" is wrong. The shortfall ratio is identical at both sizes (1/10 and 10/100), and hiring recall at 10% is 22% at both sizes without noise.

## Where the false alerts come from (paid hours)

Generated by `work/decompose_noise_v04.py`. "Planted" is the v0.3 hidden-external-efficiency case, which alerts in essentially every seed by design.

| Size | Demand σ | Hours σ | Cutoff | Planted false alerts | Noise-driven false alerts / other nulls | Cases involved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.03 | 0 | 10% | 39 | 0 / 265 | none |
| 10 | 0.06 | 0 | 10% | 40 | 3 / 249 | demand_absorption 1, valuable_new_work 2 |
| 10 | 0.06 | 0 | 5% | 40 | 24 / 249 | demand_absorption 8, high_retention_value 4, valuable_new_work 12 |
| 10 | 0 | 0.03 | 5% | 40 | 19 / 280 | demand_absorption 6, high_retention_value 6, valuable_new_work 7 |
| 100 | 0.03 | 0 | 10% | 40 | 0 / 232 | none |
| 100 | 0.06 | 0 | 10% | 39 | 5 / 226 | demand_absorption 3, valuable_new_work 2 |
| 100 | 0.06 | 0 | 5% | 39 | 17 / 226 | demand_absorption 9, valuable_new_work 8 |
| 100 | 0 | 0.03 | 5% | 40 | 17 / 280 | demand_absorption 5, high_retention_value 6, valuable_new_work 6 |

**The key structural finding:** every noise-driven false alert occurs in a null history that *adopted AI* (valuable new work, demand absorption, high retention value). These firms genuinely replaced tasks, so the Level 1 task-evidence gate is open, and any noisy dip relative to the comparator is attributed to AI. The `no_adoption` null never false-alerts under any noise setting, because no task replacement is published and the gate stays closed. The task-evidence prerequisite protects against blaming AI where it was not used. It does not protect against blaming AI for noise where it was used. That is exactly where real monitoring would be applied.

## Selected metrics (10% cutoff, immediate publication)

| Size | Demand σ | Hours recall | Hours false-alert rate | Hiring recall | Hiring false-alert rate | Layoffs recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0 | 89% | 12% | 22% | 0% | 71% |
| 10 | 0.03 | 86% | 13% | 36% | 2% | 68% |
| 10 | 0.06 | 83% | 15% | 47% | 9% | 58% |
| 100 | 0 | 89% | 12% | 22% | 0% | 71% |
| 100 | 0.03 | 78% | 15% | 46% | 7% | 70% |
| 100 | 0.06 | 78% | 17% | 52% | 12% | 65% |

Hiring recall *rises* with noise, but so do its false alerts: noise moves cumulative hiring ratios across the cutoff in both directions. This is not an improvement in the indicator. Denominators: positives and negatives change across noise settings because shocks alter which histories truly have an adverse AI effect (for example 360 → 376 → 391 hours positives at size 10).

## Judgement

The pre-declared weakening criterion (hours false alerts above about 20% of nulls at 10% with σ_d = 0.03, or recall below half) was **not met**. Paid hours remains the best quantity candidate. The 5% cutoff should be dropped as a default: it is where noise starts to dominate. Fixed single-comparator gap rules survive mild i.i.d. noise but need explicit uncertainty before any real use, particularly for firms that have adopted AI.

## Limitations

Shocks are i.i.d. and mean-reverting; real demand is persistent, which would let a comparator drift and is likely to be much harsher. One comparator only. Pay is still fixed at 25/hour, so wage erosion remains untested. Shock sizes are illustrative, not calibrated. All cases and rules were visible during development; there is no holdout. Seeds measure within-model randomness, not model validity.

The plan said `results.md` would be generated in the output folder. The runner instead writes `manifest.json` and `metrics.csv`; interpretation lives here. Raw per-seed histories are not saved; they regenerate deterministically from the manifest.

## Next experiments

1. **Persistent shocks** (AR(1) or random-walk demand): the most likely way to break the 10% rule.
2. **Noise-aware attribution:** require the gap to exceed a band estimated from pre-adoption comparator variance (q0–q2, or a longer pre-period), or use several comparators. Test whether this removes the adopter-null false alerts without collapsing recall.
3. **Wage setting:** endogenous pay so the wage-erosion outcome has true positives at all.
